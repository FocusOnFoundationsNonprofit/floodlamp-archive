## Context
The `various/papers-lamp` subcategory contains a curated selection of published papers on RT-LAMP (reverse transcription loop-mediated isothermal amplification) tests for SARS-CoV-2 detection. The collection includes work from academic groups and commercial entities whose efforts were particularly relevant to FloodLAMP's development and operations during the pandemic. This is not a comprehensive literature review — it is a somewhat haphazard selection of papers that intersected directly with FloodLAMP's work or that represent notable milestones in LAMP-based COVID-19 testing.

All papers in this subcategory are in pdf format and were not converted to markdown. Citations are available in `_citations_papers-lamp.md`. For a more comprehensive list of LAMP-related papers, see the gLAMP Consortium review paper in `various/glamp` (`gLAMP Global Consortium - JBT Review Paper (Sept 2021)`), which includes an extensive bibliography; note that the review was published in September 2021 and its references likely cover literature only through mid-2021, excluding later pandemic papers and post-pandemic LAMP-related retrospectives.


## Commentary
The foundational paper in the collection is Rabe and Cepko (2020, PNAS) `Rabe Cepko - SARS-CoV-2 detection using isothermal amplification (7-8-2020 PNAS Resub)`, which describes the assay that FloodLAMP adopted as the basis for its testing protocol. The Rabe-Cepko test offered both a purified version, using a low-cost "glass milk" silica purification method, and a direct (extraction-free) version. FloodLAMP initially used the purified version before transitioning to the direct protocol for its subsequent clinical validation and FDA submissions as well as all of its surveillance pilot testing programs. A companion clinical validation paper (Anahtar, Rabe, et al., 2021) `Rabe Cepko Anahtar - Clinical Assessment and Validation (12-21-2020)` further established the assay's performance. In this archive's `various/lamp-tech` subcategory there is additional technical background on this assay, including an interview with Brian Rabe about the test's development.

Several papers represent groups that were central to the open-source LAMP testing community. The Kellner et al. paper from the Vienna BioCenter (2020) `Kellner (Vienna) - Preprint - Open LAMP SARS-CoV-2 assay (7-23-2020)` describes an open RT-LAMP assay developed by a group that was a prominent contributor to the Global LAMP Consortium (gLAMP). The Vienna group established rtlamp.org, a resource hosting open protocols, background information, how-to guides, and eventually distributing test kits. The `various/glamp` subcategory of this archive provides more information. Dave O'Connor's group at the University of Wisconsin contributed multiple papers on direct RT-LAMP evaluation and optimization `OConnor - Preprint - Initial evaluation of a mobile SARS-CoV-2 RT-LAMP testing strategy (7-28-2020)` `OConnor Dudley - Preprint - Optimizing direct RT-LAMP(8-30-2020)`. O'Connor also shared surveillance program documentation that is cross-referenced in the archive subcategory `regulatory/surveillance`. Nathan Tanner and colleagues at New England Biolabs (NEB) published papers on both foundational LAMP methodology and practical applications, including an extraction-free saliva RT-LAMP workflow for workplace surveillance conducted in partnership with Mirimus `Tanner (NEB) - Current Protocols Loop-Mediated Isothermal Amplification for Detection of Nucleic Acids (2014)` `Tanner (NEB and Mirimus) - Preprint - Extraction-Free Saliva SARS-CoV-2 RT-LAMPWorkflow for Workplace Surveillance (3-11-2022)`. Tanner was a key contributor to the gLAMP community, and NEB's WarmStart Colorimetric LAMP 2X Master Mix was a critical reagent for FloodLAMP and many other LAMP-based testing efforts.

On the commercial side, Color Genomics obtained FDA Emergency Use Authorization for a purified, fluorometric LAMP assay read on a PCR thermal cycler `Color Whitepaper - SARS-CoV-2 LAMP Diagnostic Assay (5-21-2020)`. Color's test was widely deployed in screening programs across the San Francisco Bay Area, including at Stanford University and numerous companies. Its authorization and scale of deployment served as an important validation that LAMP-based molecular testing could function as a practical alternative to RT-PCR in large screening programs. The collection also includes a paper from Domus `Domus Paper - A lyophilized colorimetric RT-LAMP test kit (2022 Nature)`, which developed a lyophilized colorimetric RT-LAMP kit. Domus was a recipient of late-stage RADx (Rapid Acceleration of Diagnostics) funding from NIH.

Christopher Mason's group at Weill Cornell Medicine validated a fast (30-minute) colorimetric LAMP test for SARS-CoV-2 as part of a broader multi-omics study (Butler, Mozsary, Meydan, et al., 2021, Nature Communications) `Butler Mason (Weill Cornell) - Shotgun transcriptome, spatial omics, and isothermal profiling of SARS-CoV-2 (2021 Nat Commun)`. Mason developed this LAMP assay in collaboration with Color Genomics and New England Biolabs; the test achieved 96% sensitivity and 99% specificity compared to RT-PCR and formed the basis of Color's FDA Emergency Use Authorization in May 2020. The preprint (April 2020) was one of the earliest LAMP clinical validation studies. Mason, who is from Racine, Wisconsin, also led a community LAMP testing pilot there in the summer of 2020, working with local firefighters and in collaboration with David O'Connor's group at UW-Madison; this initiative was covered in Wired and Spectrum News but to the best of our knowledge was not published as a standalone paper.

Additional papers in the collection represent other notable academic efforts in LAMP-based SARS-CoV-2 detection, including work from Stanford (Prakash, 2020, using the Rabe-Cepko protocol with a low-cost centrifuge) `Prakash (Stanford) - Preprint - Rabe Cepko LAMP w Handyfuge (6-30-20)`, UPenn (Sherrill-Mix, 2020, on molecular beacons) `Sherrill-Mix (UPenn) - Preprint - LAMP Molecular Beacons (8-13-2020)` and (Song, 2020, on closed-tube detection) `Song (UPenn) - Preprint - Closed Tube Molecular COVID-19 TestRAMP assay (2-19-2020)`, University of Chicago (Wang, 2022, saliva-based assays) `Wang (UI Chicago) - Developing RT-LAMP assays for rapid diagnosis of SARS-CoV-2 in saliva (2022)`, and groups working in sub-Saharan Africa (Marcello et al., multicenter evaluation) `Marcello - Diagnostic performance of a colorimetric RT -LAMP in sub-Saharan Africa`. An AI-generated literature review included in the subcategory, `_AI_Literature Review - LAMP Large-Scale Testing and Pandemic Preparedness`, extends coverage to 20 additional papers prioritized for large-scale testing and pandemic preparedness. The subcategory also includes a citations file, `_citations_papers-lamp`, with full bibliographic references and links for all papers in the collection.
